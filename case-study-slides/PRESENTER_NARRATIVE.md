## 7. AI-assisted transformation of airline crew operations

### Story

This international-airline case is about accelerating change across deeply interconnected crew operations within a broader airline application estate. The modernization connected five application domains covering scheduling, payroll, profile management, training, and accommodation. Engineers analyzed 6.2 million lines of code across the broader estate, mapped more than 52,000 data interactions, and addressed more than 2,500 epics without breaking the operational controls that keep crews moving.

The delivery team first mapped code, process flows, business rules, and dependencies. It then added two simple capabilities: a domain-context layer containing crew rules and system relationships, and an engineering harness that controlled changes, ran automated checks, and preserved review evidence. These capabilities supported repeatable analysis, design, code transformation, and verification.

The results are a reduction in program delivery from 3.5 years to 1.25 years and approximately 50% engineering acceleration. Grounded truth connected rules to their source, while traceability linked change evidence throughout delivery.

### Customer takeaway

AI can accelerate a high-risk operational environment when it understands the domain, follows the engineering rules, and proves what changed. The executive result is not merely faster code generation; it is a shorter transformation timeline with crew rules, regulatory controls, and verification still in the delivery path.

### Presenter handoff

Use this case to show that domain knowledge and governed execution make AI useful in complex operational systems. Do not describe the case as legacy modernization.

## 8. AI support across a regulated banking SDLC

### Story

This banking case is about accelerating the full delivery lifecycle without separating speed from regulated assurance. PII, security, regulatory obligations, functional quality, human approvals, and audit evidence all had to remain part of the engineering path.

The bank translated regulatory, PII, and security obligations into controls that followed requirements through architecture, engineering, and quality assurance. Six families of persona-based agents supported business analysis, architecture, engineering, security, compliance, and QA. Functional, security, and compliance checks ran throughout delivery, while human approvals, least-privilege access, and traceable evidence remained in place.

The delivery model produced a 1.5x productivity gain and saved 5,000 hours. The distinctive result was continuous assurance: evidence became available during review rather than being assembled after the work.

### Customer takeaway

Regulated organizations gain more than coding speed when security, compliance, PII protection, testing, and evidence operate as one delivery system. The bank improved productivity while strengthening the controls required to release with confidence.

## 9. End-to-end AI delivery

### Story

This healthcare and life sciences organization needed five digital portals before the market window closed. A conventional 12-person scrum team would have moved one portal at a time, with each portal requiring six to eight months. Functional handoffs separated domain knowledge, engineering, quality, and compliance, while every portal repeated the same setup effort.

The organization adopted a startup operating model. Patient and administrative journeys became executable product specifications linking requirements, acceptance rules, controls, tests, and release evidence. The 12-person scrum team became four three-person outcome pods. Each pod combined a domain lead, an AI-enabled engineer, and a quality and compliance owner.

The first pod delivered a production portal in one month rather than the conventional six-to-eight-month timeline. The organization then replicated the pod structure and delivery pattern across the remaining portfolio. The model delivered approximately 5x portal speed while keeping compliance and evidence inside each pod.

### Customer takeaway

The innovation was not simply faster portal engineering. Role compression, end-to-end pod ownership, executable specifications, and parallel delivery converted one sequential scrum team into a portfolio delivery system.

## 10. Enterprise adoption at scale

### Story

This case is about turning access to a commercial AI coding product into a governed enterprise delivery system. The working scope is approximately 3,000 enabled users across 280 teams and 40 programs, with sustained usage of approximately one billion model tokens per month.

The burden was not a single failing application or a legacy-modernization problem. The constraint shifted as adoption expanded: each team had different access to business rules, code, architecture, standards, tools, and delivery controls. MCP-enabled workflows required enterprise authentication, approved scopes, and traceable execution. Leaders also needed quality, support, model routing, usage, and cost to operate as one portfolio system rather than as local team practices.

The organization standardized a commercial AI product, approved models, and engineering tools as the foundation. It then added a context and engineering harness layer containing trusted enterprise context, MCP identity and scopes, verification and evidence, release recovery, model routing, and cost observability. AI-generated work progressed through acceptance, security, risk, and traceability gates. Shared workflows, enablement, and controls were then rolled out across the portfolio.

The working results are a reduction in portfolio-increment cycle time from six months to two months, sprint feedback from two weeks to one week, pipeline-failure diagnosis to less than five minutes, and a 58% reduction in mean time to recovery. The slide treats user, team, program, and token counts as proof of operating scale; the delivery and quality measures remain the proof of value.

### Customer takeaway

Enterprise adoption becomes credible when faster delivery, verification, recovery, identity, usage, and cost controls scale together. Tool licenses and token volume describe the operating load. They do not prove value without accepted delivery outcomes and quality evidence.

## 11. AI-native eDiscovery delivery

### Story

The legal technology organization depended on manual interviews to identify data sources, page-by-page keyword review, and manual redaction and production. These activities consumed specialist capacity and limited the number of matters the organization could handle consistently.

The organization built an AI-native eDiscovery platform covering discovery, review, redaction, and case preparation. Hybrid delivery pods combined a 120-engineer organization with AI agents. The platform automated high-volume work while specialists reviewed exceptions, and the workflow preserved audit evidence and defensible output.

The platform reduced review costs by 40–60%, reached 77% review precision, and reduced documents requiring human review from 25–30% to approximately 5%.

### Customer takeaway

The customer converted manual legal effort into a productized, scalable workflow without removing specialist judgment from the decisions that require it.

## 12. Faster policy servicing, from code to customer answer

### Story

This large-insurer case connects AI-assisted software delivery with the quality of policy-servicing answers. Policy logic, customer questions, and advisor registration lived across different systems and teams. Changes were slow, while routine queries and administration consumed people who could otherwise handle exceptions and higher-value work.

The insurer used AI-assisted engineering to improve the policy-servicing application and reduce build and debugging lead time. A knowledge assistant made approved policy knowledge available during customer-query resolution. Advisor registration and administrative workflows were automated. The proposed case-study frame connects these three investments as one operating story: faster change in the product, more consistent answers in service, and less manual processing in operations. Policy grounding, human escalation, and release checks are recommended assurance elements to validate with the account team.

The supplied deck reports a 90% improvement in policy-servicing query resolution, a 50% reduction in manual or human query resolution, and a 40% improvement in AI-assisted delivery cycles. The exact denominators, reporting period, and comparison method require confirmation before external publication.

### Customer takeaway

The distinctive result is not just faster engineering. Reusable policy knowledge can improve both how quickly the insurer changes its software and how consistently it serves customers, while automation reduces routine administrative work.

## Overview presenter handoff

Use slide 06 before the individual cases. Read each tile as a different constraint: connected airline operations; regulated banking assurance; sequential healthcare portal delivery; enterprise AI adoption; specialist legal review; and insurance policy knowledge. The shared pattern is trusted domain context, AI-supported execution, and verification within the delivery flow. Do not imply that one metric or operating model applies to every industry.

## Narrative pattern for every customer case

Present each case in this sequence:

1. Establish the business and engineering problem.
2. Quantify the scale or define the operating scope and complexity.
3. Explain how context, AI, people, and controls worked together.
4. Show the change in delivery flow.
5. Prove that quality and governance held or improved.
6. Connect AI and operating cost to a verified outcome.
7. State what the customer should learn from the case.

Avoid leading with prompts, generated code, tokens, agent calls, tool licenses, or training attendance. These measures explain activity. Customer value requires an accepted outcome, a quality measure, and an economic denominator.
