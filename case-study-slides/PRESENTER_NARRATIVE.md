## 7. Engineering harness for a complex environment

### Story

This case is about forward engineering in a large, interconnected, modern environment. The challenge was not simply generating code. Engineers needed to understand millions of lines of code, more than 50,000 data interactions, business rules, system dependencies, and the verification obligations surrounding every change.

The organization created a context layer that connects code, architecture, data flows, engineering standards, tests, and prior decisions. An engineering harness uses that context to shape AI-generated changes, constrain execution, run required checks, and produce evidence for human review.

### Customer takeaway

AI becomes dependable when it can understand the environment, follow its engineering rules, and prove what it changed. The harness reduces discovery and impact-analysis effort while increasing first-pass verification and reducing rework.

### Presenter handoff

Use this case to transition into the broader context-engineering and AI-harness story: model capability alone does not create a trustworthy delivery system.

## 8. Coordinated transformation across technologies

### Story

This transformation spans custom applications, packaged products, Java or platform upgrades, data services, and cloud integrations. Each workstream has a different engineering method, but the business outcome depends on all of them moving together.

The delivery model treats the work as one portfolio. Shared context exposes dependencies across systems. AI supports code changes, package analysis, upgrade planning, interface mapping, testing, and release preparation. Program-level controls coordinate milestones, acceptance criteria, and evidence across teams.

### Customer takeaway

Local engineering speed does not guarantee transformation speed. The important result is lower cross-team waiting, earlier dependency discovery, fewer integration defects, and more predictable integrated releases.

## 9. End-to-end AI delivery

### Story

This startup-like environment must deliver five digital portals with a lean internal team and limited external support. The objective is to use AI across the entire lifecycle rather than only during coding.

AI helps structure requirements, identify gaps, create experience concepts, implement reusable components, generate tests, prepare release evidence, and support deployment. Requirements remain linked to designs, code, tests, approvals, and production outcomes. Reusable assets allow later portals to benefit from the work completed for the first release.

### Customer takeaway

The strongest proof is not the number of generated artifacts. It is the time to the first production portal, the acceleration of later portals, team leverage, reuse, production quality, and real user adoption.

### Evidence still required

Confirm the five portal names, which portal has been released, release timing, team size, remaining scope, external support, user adoption, and production results.

## 10. Enterprise adoption at scale

### Story

This case moves beyond a proof of concept to enterprise adoption involving approximately 2,000 participants. At this scale, access to an AI tool is only the beginning. The organization needs an operating model for enablement, support, governance, measurement, model usage, and cost management.

Users progress from enabled, to active, to sustained, productive use. Teams apply approved workflows and measure their effect on cycle time, throughput, quality, and rework. Platform teams observe token and model usage, route work to appropriate models, enforce budgets, and connect cost to accepted delivery outcomes.

### Customer takeaway

Enterprise adoption should be measured through sustained productive use, active teams and programs, workflow penetration, time to proficiency, quality, and cost per verified outcome. Total tokens and licenses describe scale, but they do not prove value.

### Disclosure guardrail

Validate the participant definition, reporting period, program and team counts, productivity method, token volume, cost, tool association, and disclosure permission. Do not publish a billion- or trillion-token claim without a governed source and a meaningful outcome denominator.

## 11. Distinct legal-technology use case

### Illustrative working story

The current transcript does not contain enough evidence to state an actual customer claim. A differentiated working scenario is AI-assisted matter intake and workspace preparation.

New matters arrive through documents, email, spreadsheets, and client systems. Specialists manually identify parties, jurisdictions, deadlines, custodians, data sources, access rules, and retention obligations before configuring a matter workspace.

AI classifies the intake package, extracts required information, identifies missing or conflicting fields, and proposes a matter model and workspace configuration. The system checks the proposal against client policies and prior approved matters. A legal specialist retains approval authority, and the system preserves a source-to-decision audit trail.

### Customer takeaway

This is distinct from a general document-review or eDiscovery story. The outcome is faster, more complete matter setup with less manual re-entry, while legal specialists retain control over activation.

### Evidence still required

The content owner must provide the actual starting condition, AI approach, delivery model, baseline, measurable outcome, and lesson learned. Until then, the scenario and all associated metrics remain illustrative.

## Narrative pattern for every customer case

Present each case in this sequence:

1. Establish the business and engineering problem.
2. Quantify the scale and complexity.
3. Explain how context, AI, people, and controls worked together.
4. Show the change in delivery flow.
5. Prove that quality and governance held or improved.
6. Connect AI and operating cost to a verified outcome.
7. State what the customer should learn from the case.

Avoid leading with prompts, generated code, tokens, agent calls, tool licenses, or training attendance. These measures explain activity. Customer value requires an accepted outcome, a quality measure, and an economic denominator.
